Culture

Sudanese culture brings together diverse languages, traditions, and ways of life. From shared meals and coffee gatherings to music, poetry, and traditional clothing, culture connects people to their communities and their roots.

Podcast Transcript: Exploring Sudan

[00:03] Introductions

Keira: Hello, and welcome back to our podcast! We are the Trailblazers. My name is Keira, and my fellow group members will introduce themselves.

Nawaf: Hello, my name is Nawaf, and I’m from Sudan.

Cindy: Hi, my name is Cindy, and I’m from Rwanda.

Sylvie: Hi, my name is Sylvie, and I’m from Rwanda.

Delice: Hi, my name is Delice, and I’m from Rwanda.

Keira: Today, we’re going to talk about Sudan as our chosen community. It’s a very interesting country, and we have a lot of information to share about it.

[00:42] Why We Chose Sudan

Keira: Let’s start with why our group chose Sudan.

We chose Sudan because it’s a country that many people know little about. Yet it has so much to discuss: interesting facts, misconceptions, and unique qualities that many people don’t know about.

Cindy is going to tell us more about some of these facts.

[01:14] Sudan’s Diversity

Cindy: Sudan is a very diverse country, with [number unclear] languages spoken across its communities. Imagine visiting one community where people speak one language, then going to another where people speak a different language. It’s very diverse.

One interesting thing I noticed when coming to university is that I would see people and think they were Sudanese. But you can’t always tell by appearance alone. You might only realise they’re Sudanese when you hear them speaking Arabic, which is a common language there.

[01:44] Misconceptions About Sudan

Keira: Thank you, Cindy, for sharing those facts about Sudan. Now we’re going to discuss some misconceptions, and Delice is going to help us understand them.

Delice: Thank you. One misconception is that many people confuse Sudan with South Sudan. I think this is because they have similar names and used to be one country before they separated.

Another reason is their shared history and culture, as well as their geographical proximity. These are some of the reasons people confuse Sudan with South Sudan.

Another misconception concerns women. Many people think women in Sudan don’t attend school or receive an education. However, women’s education began in 1907. Many women attend university, receive an education, and hold high positions in government. Women first began attending university in 1930.

Another misconception is that Egypt has the most pyramids, whereas Sudan has more pyramids than Egypt.

Thank you.

Keira: That’s very interesting! Sudan has more pyramids than Egypt, which I think many of our listeners may not have known.

[03:38] Challenges Facing Sudan

Keira: We’re lucky to have a Sudanese member in our group: Nawaf. We’d like him to tell us more about Sudan and some of the problems the country faces.

Nawaf: Different parts of Sudan face many problems. In the west, east, and north, for example, conflicts between tribes are common.

The main issue now is—well, I wouldn’t describe it simply as a conflict. There is a militia trying to take control of Sudan and rule the country, while the military is trying to defend Sudan from it.

Keira: What is the conflict about?

Nawaf: It’s about ruling the country, controlling resources, and gaining power over those resources. It’s a long story, but we’ll try to keep it brief because we only have three minutes left.

Keira: Thank you so much, Nawaf.

Nawaf: Of course.

Keira: He’s also trying to learn Kinyarwanda, which is lovely. We’re glad to have him in our group.

Nawaf: Thank you.

[05:04] Unique Places to Visit in Sudan

Keira: The last thing we’re going to discuss is what makes Sudan unique. Sudan has many unique features that people often don’t know about. Let’s hear more about them.

Speaker: Thank you. I’m going to talk about some unique places you can visit in Sudan.

Let’s start with the Red Sea. It lies along Sudan’s coast, and I’m sure you’ve heard of it. There are coral reefs in the Red Sea, which the speaker describes as “red plants.” They’re very beautiful, and you can visit the area to see them.

There is also a marine national park called Sanganeb [name interpreted from pronunciation]. It’s a beautiful place for people who enjoy diving and seeing different marine animals.

If you’re visiting the Red Sea coast and want something to drink, there are also coffee shops where you can stop for a coffee.

The last thing I’d like to mention is the desert. Parts of Sudan lie within the Sahara Desert. If you’ve never seen a desert, you can visit and experience what it’s like. You can see the pyramids, too.

You should all visit these places!

[06:50] Closing Remarks

Keira: That’s very true. Sudan is a fascinating country that people don’t always appreciate for what it is. It’s a beautiful country, and I think we should all visit, right?

Group: Yes!

Keira: Nawaf is going to take us there, right?

Speaker: Yes, he’s going to be our guide.

Nawaf: If you want to visit Sudan, just call me!

Keira: Thank you all for staying tuned. See you next time!

Group: See you next time!
